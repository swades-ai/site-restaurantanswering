## The short answer

A restaurant that had been letting calls ring out during peak hours put RestaurantAnswering on the line and stopped losing orders it never knew it was losing. RestaurantAnswering is an AI answering service for restaurants that answers every call 24/7, takes phone orders, books reservations and manages waitlists without adding staff.

The rest of this page is what that looks like in practice, so you can decide whether it fits your operation.

## The problem the phone creates

A ringing phone during a Friday dinner rush is a decision, not a task. Someone has to stop plating, stop seating, or stop running food to pick it up, and the calls that arrive while nobody can pick up do not come back later. They go to the next restaurant in the search results.

That is the gap RestaurantAnswering is built for. It answers in under a second and recovers revenue from missed and after-hours calls, according to the company's own published figures. The mechanism is simple: the call gets answered by something that already knows your menu, so no one leaves the line to answer it.

## What the AI actually handles

RestaurantAnswering answers every call 24/7 — phone orders, reservations, waitlists, menu and hours questions, catering and after-hours coverage — without adding staff. That list matters more than the label, because most restaurants do not have one phone problem, they have five.

- **Phone orders.** The caller orders, the AI confirms items, modifiers and totals, and the order lands where your kitchen already looks.
- **Reservations.** Bookings are taken against real availability rather than a message someone transcribes later. See [Capture reseravations](/capture-reseravations/).
- **Waitlists.** Callers are added to the list and told what to expect, which cuts the callback loop. See [High no show solution](/high-no-show-solution/).
- **Menu and hours questions.** The single most common call type, and the one that wastes the most staff time.
- **Catering and after-hours.** Orders that arrive when no one is in the building. See [Catering management](/catering-management/) and [After hour call handling](/after-hour-call-handling/).

## How it connects to what you already run

The system is restaurant voice AI that connects directly with your POS, reservation platforms and phone provider, using real menu items, pricing, modifiers and availability in real time. That last clause is the whole product. An answering service that reads a static script will quote a price you changed in March and book a table you do not have.

Because it reads live data, the same call can be answered correctly at 12:40pm and at 11:40pm, on a day when you 86'd the special and on a day you did not. The [Integrations](/integrations/) page lists what it plugs into. If your stack is unusual, that is the page to check before anything else.

## A comparison that matters

| | Staff answering | Voicemail | RestaurantAnswering |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answers during peak | Only if someone is free | No | Yes |
| Answers after close | No | No | Yes |
| Knows today's menu and pricing | Usually | No | Yes, from the POS |
| Adds headcount | Yes | No | No |
| Cost model | Hourly wage | Free, and you lose the order | Per minute, metered |

The honest read: voicemail is free and costs you the order. A person is better at a difficult caller and worse at 11pm. The AI sits in the middle and covers the hours people cannot.

## What it costs

Plans start at $29/month for 100 minutes included, with volume-based pricing as low as $0.15/month. Minutes beyond the plan are metered, so a restaurant that gets 40 calls a week and a restaurant that gets 400 are not on the same bill.

Two things to check before you commit. First, count your actual call minutes for a week — not calls, minutes — because a 90-second order and a 6-minute catering enquiry are not the same unit. Second, decide what happens to calls the AI cannot resolve; the answer should be a transfer to a person, not a dropped line.

## Where it fits and where it does not

It fits quick service, fast casual and multi-location groups whose phones overwhelm staff during peak hours. Multi-location groups get the additional benefit of every location answering the same way, which is the subject of [Franchise call inconsistency](/franchise-call-inconsistency/).

It fits less well if your calls are mostly complex complaints that need a manager's judgement, or if your menu changes hourly in ways your POS does not track. In those cases the AI still answers, but a human still has to own the outcome.

## Compliance and data

The Enterprise plan carries SOC 2, HIPAA & GDPR compliance. If you take catering orders that include health-related dietary detail, or you are part of a group with a security review, that is the plan to ask about rather than the entry tier.

## What to do next

Pull last month's call log and count the calls that rang out or hit voicemail after close. That number, multiplied by your average ticket, is the case for or against this. If it is small, you do not need it yet. If it is not, the [Home](/) page is where the plans and the demo sit.
