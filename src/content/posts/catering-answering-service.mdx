A catering answering service answers the calls your team cannot reach during service and turns them into quoted, dated orders instead of voicemails. RestaurantAnswering is an AI answering service for restaurants that answers calls 24/7, takes phone orders, books reservations and manages waitlists without adding staff, and it handles catering enquiries with the same scope: headcounts, per-person pricing, delivery windows and lead times.

Catering is the call type where a missed ring costs the most. The ticket is many times a dine-in cover, the caller is usually ordering for a date weeks out, and they will dial the next restaurant on their list rather than leave a message. A dropped catering call is not a lost table, it is a lost event.

## What a catering answering service actually does

A catering answering service picks up when the line is busy, when the kitchen is mid-rush and when the building is dark. The difference between a generic answering service and a restaurant-specific one is what happens after the greeting: a generic service takes a message, while a restaurant system can quote from your real menu, apply your real lead times and put the order into the pipeline your kitchen already reads.

For catering specifically, the call types that matter are:

- Large party orders placed days or weeks ahead, with a pickup or delivery time attached
- Headcount and per-person budget questions that need a real quote, not a callback
- Dietary and allergy questions on a set menu, which need a scripted, consistent answer
- Delivery radius, setup, chafing dish and staffing questions
- Date-availability checks for a specific Saturday in a specific month
- Follow-ups on a quote already sent, where the caller wants to add ten covers

Catering callers ask compound questions in a single breath, and a system that can only capture a name and number will lose the order to whoever answers properly. The list above is the minimum scope you should test any provider against before you sign.

## How the AI handles a catering call

Restaurant voice AI that connects directly with your POS, reservation platforms and phone provider, using real menu items, pricing, modifiers and availability in real time. That integration is what separates a quote from a message. If the system cannot see that your half-tray of lasagna is sold out for the 14th, it will promise something the kitchen cannot deliver.

A typical catering call runs in this order:

1. The caller asks about catering for a date and a headcount.
2. The system checks the date against your calendar and your lead time.
3. It reads back package options with per-person pricing from your menu.
4. It captures dietary restrictions and flags them against your [allergy script template](/allergy-script-template/).
5. It confirms pickup or delivery, then writes the order to your POS or sends it to your catering inbox.

After the call, the order should appear where your team already works, not in a separate portal someone has to remember to check. Ask any provider to show you the order landing in your own system before you sign, and confirm who gets notified when a catering order arrives overnight.

## What it costs and how the minutes work

Plans start at $29, and the meter is what drives the bill rather than the seat count. Catering calls are longer than order calls, so the tier that fits a busy dine-in line may not fit a restaurant taking catering volume.

Catering calls are longer than order calls. A dine-in phone order might run 90 seconds; a catering enquiry with a quote, a date check and dietary notes can run four to six minutes. Budget on call length, not call count.

| Call type | Typical length | Why it runs long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dine-in phone order | Short | Menu items, modifiers, pickup time |
| Reservation | Short | Date, time, party size, name |
| Catering quote | Long | Headcount, per-person pricing, date availability |
| Catering follow-up | Medium | Changes to an existing order |

If catering is a meaningful share of your revenue, model the minutes before you pick a tier. A restaurant taking fifteen catering calls a week at five minutes each is spending roughly five hours of talk time a month on catering alone, which is well past the entry tier. Compare that against what your host spends on the phone during a Friday rush, and against the [high volume call handling](/high-volume-call-handling/) problem in general.

## Catering answering service vs. a human answering service

A human answering service charges per call or per minute and hands you a message. A restaurant AI answering service charges per minute and can complete the order. The trade is real in both directions: humans handle unusual requests and upset callers with more nuance, while AI never puts a caller on hold, never calls in sick and never forgets to ask about the delivery address.

Where the AI wins on catering is consistency. Every caller gets asked the same questions in the same order, which is exactly what you want when the answer involves allergens. Where a human wins is the genuinely strange request, a wedding for 200 with a custom menu, which should route to a person anyway.

Most restaurants end up with both: AI answering the routine catering volume, and a rule that anything above a set headcount or below a set lead time transfers to the catering manager. Set that threshold before you go live, not after.

## Setting it up for catering specifically

Setup is mostly menu and rules work, and it is the part that decides whether the system is useful. You need your catering menu loaded with per-person pricing, your lead times per item, your delivery radius, your blackout dates and your deposit rules.

- Load the catering menu separately from the dine-in menu, with per-person and per-tray pricing.
- Set lead times per item, not one blanket rule.
- Set a headcount ceiling above which the call transfers to a person.
- Add blackout dates for holidays and sold-out weekends.
- Write the dietary questions into the script, using the [catering script template](/catering-script-template/) as a starting point.
- Decide where the order lands: POS, email or your [catering management](/catering-management/) workflow.

The rules are what stop the AI from promising a date you cannot serve, and they are the first thing to review after the first week of live calls. Read the transcripts from week one and fix the gaps while the volume is still small enough to audit by hand.

The catering calls you lose most often are the ones that arrive outside service. A corporate booker researches on a Sunday evening; a parent planning a birthday calls at 9pm. If your phone rings out, that order goes to the restaurant that answered.

## Data handling and compliance

Catering calls carry customer names, phone numbers, delivery addresses, event dates and sometimes corporate billing details. Ask any provider where that data lives and who can see it.

SOC 2, HIPAA & GDPR compliance on the Enterprise plan. Compliance on a higher tier is common in this category, so if you handle corporate or healthcare-adjacent catering, check which tier you need before comparing headline prices.

## Questions to ask before you buy

- Can the system quote from my catering menu, or does it only take a message?
- Does it write the order into my POS, or into a separate dashboard?
- What happens when a caller asks for a date inside my lead time?
- How does it handle an allergy question it has no scripted answer for?
- What is the overage rate once I pass my included minutes?
- Can I listen to call recordings and read transcripts?
- What is the notice period, and can I run it seasonally?

The answers to these questions separate a system you can trust with a large catering order from one you cannot. Get the answers in writing, and test with a real call to your own number before you commit to a term.
