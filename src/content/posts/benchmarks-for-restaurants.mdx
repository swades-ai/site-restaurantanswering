## The benchmark numbers, stated plainly

RestaurantAnswering publishes two figures that matter when you are comparing phone answering options: it recovers **$30k+ in annual revenue** from missed and after-hours calls, and it answers at 95% order accuracy in under one second. Those are the vendor's own claims, not an independent audit, so treat them as the target you are measuring against rather than a guarantee.

The useful question is not whether those numbers are impressive. It is whether your restaurant's missed-call volume is large enough that recovering it changes your year. Below are the benchmarks to check before you buy anything.

## What a missed call actually costs

A missed call is not one lost order. It is one lost order plus the customer who calls your competitor next, plus the regular who stops trying.

To size it, pull three numbers from your own phone system: total calls per week, calls that ring out or hit voicemail during service hours, and your average ticket. Multiply missed calls by average ticket by 52. Most quick service and fast casual operators who do this exercise find the annual figure sits in the tens of thousands, which is the range RestaurantAnswering claims to recover.

After-hours calls behave differently from peak-hour calls. A caller at 11pm is usually ordering for tomorrow or asking about a reservation, not walking in. Those calls are cheap to capture and easy to lose permanently, because there is no second chance once the caller hangs up. Our [after hour call handling](/after-hour-call-handling/) page covers how those calls get routed and logged.

## Response time and accuracy benchmarks

Two operational numbers decide whether an answering system is usable in a restaurant.

**Response time.** Under one second is the bar RestaurantAnswering claims. Anything slower and callers hear dead air, assume a bad connection, and hang up. Compare this against your current hold time, which during a Friday dinner rush is usually measured in minutes, not seconds.

**Order accuracy.** 95% is the claimed figure. The remaining 5% is where your cost lives: a wrong modifier, a missing side, a remake. Ask any vendor how they handle a misheard item, because the recovery path matters more than the headline rate. Menu items, pricing, modifiers and availability have to be read in real time, which is why the system [connects directly with your POS](/integrations/) rather than running off a static menu file.

## Cost benchmarks: per minute versus per hour

This is where the comparison usually turns. Staffed phone coverage is priced per hour; AI answering is priced per minute.

RestaurantAnswering's published pricing starts at $29/month for the Starter plan with 100 minutes included, and volume pricing that goes **as low as $0.08/min**. Metered overage on higher tiers runs between $0.13/month and $0.15/month per minute depending on plan, and phone numbers are $1.50/month each.

| Cost line | Staffed coverage | RestaurantAnswering |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of pricing | Per hour | Per minute |
| Entry point | Hourly wage, minimum shift length | $29/month for 100 minutes |
| Marginal cost | Another shift | As low as $0.08/min at volume |
| Hours covered | Shift-dependent | 24/7 |
| Order accuracy claim | Varies by staff | 95% |

The structural difference is that a person is paid for the whole shift whether the phone rings or not, while per-minute pricing only charges for talk time. A restaurant with 200 minutes of monthly call volume pays for 200 minutes, not for 160 staffed hours.

## What to measure in your first 30 days

Set the baseline before you switch anything on, or you will have no way to tell whether the system is working.

- Missed calls per week, before and after
- After-hours calls captured, by hour
- Orders placed by phone versus other channels
- Reservation and waitlist bookings taken by phone
- Accuracy complaints traced back to a phone order

Run the same report for a month. If missed calls drop and phone-order revenue rises by more than the subscription cost, the math works. If your call volume is genuinely low, per-minute pricing will show that too, which is a useful answer in itself.

## Where the claims stop

The 95% accuracy, sub-second response and $30k+ recovery figures come from the vendor. No independent benchmark body publishes restaurant phone-answering accuracy rates, so there is nothing to check them against. SOC 2, HIPAA & GDPR compliance is listed on the Enterprise plan only, which matters if you are a multi-location group with a compliance review.

For multi-location operators, the benchmark to watch is consistency: whether every location's phone answers the same way at 6pm on a Saturday. That is covered in [franchise call inconsistency](/franchise-call-inconsistency/).
