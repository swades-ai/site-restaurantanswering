An AI answering service for a restaurant is software that picks up your phone line, talks to the caller, and completes the task — a to-go order, a table booking, a waitlist add, a menu question. RestaurantAnswering is one of these: it answers every call 24/7, including the calls that arrive after the kitchen closes.

The reason restaurants buy one is arithmetic. A call that rings out during the Friday rush is an order placed somewhere else, and a call at 11pm is a reservation nobody books. The mechanism is simple: the phone stops being a bottleneck, and the revenue that used to leak out of missed and after-hours calls stays on your books.

## What the AI actually does on a call

A restaurant phone call is rarely a conversation. It is a transaction with a small number of shapes, and the system is built around those shapes rather than open-ended chat.

When a caller asks for a table, the system checks availability and books it. When a caller orders, it reads back items, modifiers and the total. When a caller asks whether you are open on a holiday, it answers from your hours. When a caller asks whether a dish contains peanuts, it answers from your menu data. When the waitlist is surging, it takes the name and party size and tells the caller where they are in line.

- Phone orders, with menu items, pricing and modifiers pulled in real time
- Reservations booked against live availability
- Waitlist management, including quoted wait times
- Menu, hours, allergen and location questions
- Catering enquiries routed to the right person
- After-hours coverage, so the line never goes to voicemail

The claims worth testing are speed and accuracy. Ask for a sample of calls in your own category before you sign anything, and check the transcript against the ticket that reached the kitchen.

## How it connects to your POS and reservation book

An answering service that cannot see your menu is a message-taker. The difference here is that the system connects directly with your POS, reservation platforms and phone provider, so the order it takes is the order your kitchen sees.

That integration is what makes modifier handling possible. "No onions, extra sauce, gluten-free bun" only works if the AI is reading the same item tree your POS uses. It is also what makes reservation booking safe — the AI cannot double-book a table it cannot see.

Integration depth varies by platform. Before you commit, list the systems you run — POS, reservation platform, phone provider — and ask which are supported natively versus through a workaround. The [integrations page](/integrations/) is the place to start that list.

## Pricing: what you pay and what drives the bill

RestaurantAnswering prices on minutes, not on calls. Plans start at $29/month for 100 included minutes, and the per-minute rate falls as your usage grows. Above the included minutes, overage is metered per minute.

That structure matters more than the headline number, because restaurant calls are short. A to-go order is often under two minutes; a reservation is under one. A hundred minutes is roughly sixty to eighty transactions, which is a reasonable month for a single-location quick-service restaurant and a very short week for a multi-location group.

Two things to model before you buy:

- Your peak-hour call volume, not your daily average. The phone rings in bursts, and the plan has to cover the burst.
- Your after-hours share. Calls that arrive when nobody is there are the ones the service is really paying for, and they are also the ones you have no data on today.

## Where it fits against the alternatives

There are three realistic options for a restaurant that cannot keep up with the phone: hire, outsource to a human answering service, or automate.

| Option | Coverage | Cost shape | Menu and POS awareness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Add a staff member | Shifts you can cover | Hourly wage, plus turnover | Full, if trained |
| Human answering service | Usually 24/7 | Per call or per minute, higher rate | Script only, unless integrated |
| RestaurantAnswering | 24/7 | Per minute, volume-discounted | Connected to POS and reservations |

The honest trade-off is nuance. A human can handle a complaint about a wrong order with judgement; the AI routes it. A human can upsell a dessert; the AI reads the menu. The AI wins on availability and consistency, which is where most restaurants actually lose money.

If your problem is specifically the calls that arrive after close, start with [after hour call handling](/after-hour-call-handling/). If your problem is the rush, start with [high volume call handling](/high-volume-call-handling/).

## What to test before you commit

Do not evaluate this on a demo script. Evaluate it on your own menu, your own hours and your own call recordings.

1. Feed it your real menu, including modifiers and sold-out items, and place ten orders by phone.
2. Call after hours and see whether the greeting matches your brand.
3. Ask three allergen questions and check the answers against your kitchen's actual policy.
4. Book a reservation for a party size you know is tight.
5. Pull the call log and check that every order reached the POS.

RestaurantAnswering publishes [analytics and call reporting](/analytics-call-reporting/) as part of the product, which is what makes step five possible. If a vendor cannot show you the transcript and the outcome for each call, you cannot audit accuracy, and any accuracy figure is a claim you have to take on faith.

## Compliance and data handling

The system handles caller names, phone numbers, order history and, in some cases, payment details. RestaurantAnswering states SOC 2, HIPAA & GDPR compliance on the Enterprise plan — note that this is plan-specific, so if you need a compliance attestation for a franchise agreement or a payment processor, confirm which plan carries it before you sign.

For a single independent restaurant, compliance is mostly a question of where call recordings live and how long they are kept. For a multi-location group, it becomes a vendor-risk question your legal team will ask about.

## Who this is not for

If your phone rings a handful of times a day and your host can answer it, an AI answering service is an extra line item. If your menu changes daily and is not in a POS the system can read, the integration work will eat the benefit. If your callers are mostly regulars who want to talk to a specific person, automation will feel like a downgrade.

The fit is a restaurant with a phone that rings more than staff can answer, a menu that lives in a POS, and revenue leaking out of the hours nobody is standing at the host stand.

## Getting started

Setup is a phone number and a menu import. You keep your existing number and forward it, or port it. Calls route to the AI when you want them to — all hours, or only overflow and after close.
