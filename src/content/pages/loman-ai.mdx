## What Loman AI is, and what it is not

Loman AI is a voice agent product aimed at restaurants, built around answering inbound calls and taking orders. In the material available to us, Loman does not publish its per-minute or per-location pricing, so we cannot state what it costs — it is not public. What is public is the category it competes in: AI phone answering for restaurants, the same job RestaurantAnswering does.

The practical question for a restaurant operator is not which product has the better demo. It is which one answers the phone at 7:40pm on a Friday, reads the right modifier list, and writes the ticket into the POS the kitchen already uses. That is the axis this page compares on.

RestaurantAnswering is an AI answering service for restaurants that answers every call 24/7, takes phone orders, books reservations and manages waitlists without adding staff. Plans start at $29 for 100 minutes, with usage metered beyond that.

## Pricing and how the meter runs

RestaurantAnswering prices on minutes. The entry plan is $29 and includes 100 minutes; beyond that, usage is metered. There is no per-seat charge and no charge for the staff you do not hire, because the point of the product is that you do not hire them.

Loman AI's pricing is not public in the material we have, so a like-for-like cost comparison cannot be written honestly. The workable approach is to take your own call volume — total inbound minutes per week, split between peak and after-hours — and ask both vendors to quote against that number. A restaurant taking 60 calls a week at two minutes each is buying roughly 520 minutes a month; that is the figure to put in front of both.

If your volume is seasonal, metered pricing matters more than the headline plan. A [high volume call handling](/high-volume-call-handling/) setup and a quiet Tuesday do not cost the same, and a plan that forces you onto a fixed tier during a slow month is a plan you overpay for.

## Order taking and POS connection

An AI that answers the phone but cannot write the order into the system the kitchen reads is a message-taker, not an order-taker. RestaurantAnswering connects directly with your POS, reservation platforms and phone provider, using real menu items, pricing, modifiers and availability in real time. That means a caller asking whether the kitchen still has the short rib gets an answer based on the current menu, not a static script.

Order accuracy is the number that decides whether this works. RestaurantAnswering states 95% order accuracy. The failure mode to test for is not a wrong item — it is a missing modifier, an allergy note dropped between the phone call and the ticket. If your menu carries allergy risk, the [allergy script template](/allergy-script-template/) is worth reading before you configure anything, because the script you feed the agent is the script it reads back to the caller.

For operators whose problem is specifically orders that never arrive, [eliminate missing phone orders](/eliminate-missing-phone-orders/) covers the workflow end to end.

## After-hours coverage and missed calls

Most restaurant phone loss happens outside service hours: the call at 11:20pm asking about tomorrow's hours, the Sunday booking request, the catering enquiry that lands while the dining room is full. RestaurantAnswering answers every call 24/7, which is the point of after-hours coverage — calls that would otherwise ring out or hit voicemail.

Voicemail is the cheapest option and the one that loses the most. A caller who reaches a machine and wants a table for six on Saturday will often just call the next restaurant. If you are keeping voicemail for part of the day, the [after hours voicemail script template](/after-hours-voicemail-script-template/) is a stopgap, not a fix; [after hour call handling](/after-hour-call-handling/) is the fuller treatment.

Catering is the highest-value version of this problem, because a single missed catering call can be worth more than a week of dine-in covers. See [catering management](/catering-management/).

## Reservations, waitlists and integrations

Reservations and waitlists are a different job from order taking. A reservation call is short, needs a table and a time, and has to land in whatever book the host stand uses. A waitlist call is shorter still and happens when the room is loud. RestaurantAnswering handles both, and the [capture reseravations](/capture-reseravations/) page covers how bookings are written back.

The integration surface is the part to check against your own stack. If you run a reservation platform, a POS and a phone provider, all three need to be in the list. ## When to choose each

Choose RestaurantAnswering if your calls are overwhelming staff at peak, if a meaningful share of your volume is after-hours, or if you want a published entry price you can model against your own minute count before you talk to anyone. The metered structure suits restaurants with seasonal swings, and the POS connection matters most if your menu changes often.

Choose Loman AI if you have already evaluated it against your own call recordings and it fits your stack — we cannot argue against a product we cannot see the pricing for. The honest position is that both are in the same category, and the deciding evidence is your own call log: how many calls, at what hours, and how many of them currently end without an order or a booking.

Whichever you pick, run the same test. Take one week of recorded calls, feed them to the agent, and count how many produce a correct ticket. That number is the only comparison that matters.
