The best software for restaurants in 2025 is the stack that removes the specific bottleneck costing you money, not the longest feature list. For most quick service, fast casual and multi-location groups, that bottleneck is the phone: calls arrive during peak hours, staff cannot answer them, and the order or booking is lost.

## Start with the bottleneck, not the category

Restaurant software splits into four jobs: taking orders, seating guests, running the kitchen and the back office, and answering the phone. The first three are well served. The fourth is where operators most often under-invest, because the cost is invisible — a missed call does not appear on a report the way a voided ticket does.

Ask what happens on a Friday at 7pm when three calls ring at once and the line cook is plating. If the honest answer is "voicemail," that is your first purchase, ahead of anything that improves reporting.

## What phone answering software has to do

A phone system for restaurants is not a generic call centre. It has to know your menu, your modifiers, your sold-out items and your table availability at the moment of the call.

RestaurantAnswering is an AI answering service for restaurants that [answers every call 24/7](/after-hour-call-handling/) — phone orders, reservations, waitlists, menu and hours questions, catering and after-hours coverage — without adding staff. It answers in under a second at 95% order accuracy and recovers $30k+ in annual revenue from missed and after-hours calls.

The claim worth testing in a demo is integration. Restaurant voice AI that [connects directly with your POS](/integrations/), reservation platforms and phone provider, using real menu items, pricing, modifiers and availability in real time, is doing something a scripted answering service cannot. If the agent reads from a static list, it will sell you an item you 86'd an hour ago.

## What it costs, and how to compare quotes

RestaurantAnswering plans start at $29/month for 100 minutes included, with volume-based pricing as low as $0.08/min. That structure matters more than the headline number: a low base with expensive overage punishes exactly the peak-hour volume you bought the service for.

When you compare vendors, convert every quote to a cost per answered minute at your own call volume, including the months you are busiest. Ask each vendor three questions in writing:

- What is the per-minute rate after the included minutes, and does it change by plan?
- Is the phone number billed separately?
- What happens to a call the agent cannot complete — is it transferred, and to whom?

A vendor that will not answer those three in writing is telling you something.

## The rest of the stack, briefly

POS, reservations and waitlist tools are chosen on integration surface, not features. A reservation platform that does not write back to your POS leaves your host reconciling two systems at the door, and a waitlist tool that cannot text guests is a clipboard with extra steps.

For high-volume operations, the [high volume call handling](/high-volume-call-handling/) problem and the [drive thru bottlenecks](/drive-thru-bottlenecks/) problem are the same problem: a fixed number of staff facing a variable arrival rate. Software helps when it absorbs the variance, not when it adds another screen to check.

Multi-location groups should test consistency directly. [Franchise call inconsistency](/franchise-call-inconsistency/) — one store quoting a different price or hours than another — is a brand problem that shows up as a customer service problem.

## Compliance and data questions to ask

If you take payment details over the phone or handle guest data across locations, ask where call recordings and transcripts are stored and who can access them. RestaurantAnswering states SOC 2, HIPAA & GDPR compliance on the Enterprise plan — note that this is plan-specific, so if compliance is a requirement, confirm which plan you are buying before signing.

## How to run the trial

Pick two weeks that include a weekend. Route after-hours calls only, keep your daytime line as it is, and compare the count of completed orders and bookings against the same period last month. Measure three things: calls answered, orders completed, and calls that ended in a transfer to a human. If the third number is high, the agent is not configured to your menu yet.

For scripting your own voicemail fallback while you evaluate, the [after hours voicemail script template](/after-hours-voicemail-script-template/) is a useful baseline, and [analytics call reporting](/analytics-call-reporting/) is what you should expect from whichever vendor you choose.

## Where to start

If your phones are the bottleneck, start there and add the rest of the stack once calls stop going to voicemail. If your phones are fine and your table turns are the problem, buy the reservation or waitlist tool first. Buying both at once makes it impossible to tell which one worked.
