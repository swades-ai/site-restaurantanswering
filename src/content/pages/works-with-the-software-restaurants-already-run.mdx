## What an integration actually changes

RestaurantAnswering is an AI answering service for restaurants that answers every call 24/7. An integration is what lets that answer be specific to your restaurant rather than generic: the agent reads your real menu items, pricing, modifiers and availability in real time, so a caller asking whether the salmon is still on tonight gets an answer drawn from the same data your kitchen works from.

Without an integration, a voice agent can still take a message. With one, it can take an order, hold a table and put both where your staff already look.

## The three systems that matter

### POS

RestaurantAnswering connects directly with your POS so phone orders land as orders, not as notes someone retypes. Items, modifiers, sizes and current pricing come from the POS, which means the agent quotes what you charge today, including the price change you made last week. Orders that arrive while the kitchen is slammed queue the same way an in-person order does, and the ticket carries the caller's number so a callback is possible if something needs confirming.

### Reservation platforms

The agent books reservations into the platform your hosts already use, checking availability before it confirms rather than promising a table you do not have. Waitlist requests go to the same list your door staff manage, so a party that calls ahead is not invisible when they walk in. For operators who want the phone to stop being the bottleneck at the door, this is the integration that matters most.

### Phone provider

Calls route through your existing number rather than a new one customers have to learn. The agent answers in under a second, so callers do not hear dead air or a menu tree before a human-equivalent response. If you run multiple locations, each number can map to its own menu, hours and availability.

## Setup, in steps

1. Connect the phone number you already publish. Porting is not required to start; calls can forward.
2. Link your POS so menu items, pricing and modifiers sync.
3. Link your reservation platform so availability is checked before a booking is confirmed.
4. Review the menu the agent will read aloud, including how you want modifiers and substitutions phrased.
5. Set hours, holiday closures and the point at which a call should transfer to a person.
6. Run calls in parallel with your existing process for a short period, then switch over.

Most of the work is step 4. The agent is only as accurate as the menu data behind it, and a menu with three names for the same item will produce three answers.

## What this looks like in practice

A fast-casual counter with one phone line and two people on the register during the lunch rush is the clearest case. Every call answered is a call the register staff did not have to stop for, and the order arrives in the POS with modifiers intact.

A multi-location group has a different problem: the same question gets a different answer depending on who picks up. Routing each location's number to its own menu and hours removes that variation, and the call reporting shows which locations are absorbing the most call volume. See [Analytics call reporting](/analytics-call-reporting/) for what that data looks like.

After-hours is the third case. A restaurant that closes at 10pm and receives calls at 11pm is not losing a reservation, it is losing an order that a competitor will take. RestaurantAnswering recovers $30k+ in annual revenue from missed and after-hours calls, according to the company's own published figures, at 95% order accuracy.

## Pricing for integrations

Integration work is included in the plan rather than sold separately. Plans start at $29/month for 100 minutes included, with volume pricing as low as $0.08/min. Extra minutes are metered, and phone numbers are billed per number per month. If you are comparing this against hiring, the relevant comparison is not the plan price but the cost of the calls you currently miss.

## Where the data sits

Order and reservation data moves between RestaurantAnswering and your systems; it is not a separate silo your staff have to check. SOC 2, HIPAA & GDPR compliance is available on the Enterprise plan, which matters if you are part of a group with its own security review. For a single-site operator, the practical question is simpler: can you see every call, and can you export it.

## Before you connect anything

Two things decide whether an integration project goes well. First, whether your menu data is clean enough to read aloud. Second, whether your team will trust the agent enough to stop answering every call themselves. The first is technical and takes an afternoon. The second takes a week of listening to recordings.
