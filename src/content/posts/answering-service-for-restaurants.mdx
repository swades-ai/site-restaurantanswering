An answering service for restaurants picks up the calls your staff cannot: the Friday rush, the after-close order, the reservation line ringing while the host seats a party. RestaurantAnswering is an AI answering service that answers every call 24/7, takes phone orders, books reservations and manages waitlists without adding staff.

The decision is not about a friendlier greeting. It is about the calls that currently ring out, hit voicemail, or get abandoned mid-ring while someone runs food.

## What the service handles, call by call

RestaurantAnswering answers in under a second and reads the order back before confirming, per the company's own published claims. It is a conversation, not a menu tree that makes callers press 1.

The scope is the call mix a restaurant actually receives:

- Phone orders, including modifiers and substitutions
- Reservations and table bookings
- Waitlist additions and quoted wait times
- Menu, hours, location and parking questions
- Catering enquiries and large-order intake
- After-hours and closed-day coverage

All of it runs on the number already printed on your menus. Callers install nothing and learn nothing new.

## How it connects to your POS and reservation book

The technology claim that matters most is that it connects directly with your POS, reservation platforms and phone provider. That connection separates a restaurant answering service from a voicemail box with a transcript.

Because it reads real menu items, pricing, modifiers and availability in real time, it can tell a caller the soup is sold out, the 7:30 slot is gone, or the gluten-free base is available. A static script fails on the first follow-up question, and callers notice.

For multi-location groups, the same connection lets each store answer with its own menu and hours instead of a shared corporate script. See [Integrations](/integrations/) for the platforms involved and [Analytics call reporting](/analytics-call-reporting/) for what comes back after the calls end.

## What it costs and how the meter works

Pricing is by minutes, not seats or per-call fees. Plans start at $29/month for 100 included minutes, with volume pricing as low as $0.12/month. Overages are metered, so a busy month costs more than a quiet one and you do not pay for unused capacity.

Run the arithmetic on your own numbers. A single missed Friday-night order is frequently larger than a month of the entry plan. The [comparison](/comparison/) page sets the tiers side by side.

## Edge cases that decide the contract

Peak concurrency is the first one. Two hundred calls a day spread evenly is a different problem from forty calls in the same twenty minutes, and the second is what a dinner rush looks like. Ask how simultaneous calls are handled and whether any queue forms.

Language coverage is the second. A neighbourhood with a large Spanish- or Mandarin-speaking customer base needs the system to take those orders, not transfer them. Confirm which languages are supported before you count the calls it can recover.

Menu changes are the third. A daily special, a sold-out item, a seasonal price change — each has to reach the AI as fast as it reaches the POS. If your team updates availability in one system and the phone reads another, callers get yesterday's menu.

## What it does not do

It does not replace your POS, your reservation platform, or your staff. It does not handle cash reconciliation, refunds, or complaints that need a manager's judgement; those should route to a human, and you should confirm the routing rules before go-live.

It also does not fix a menu that is wrong in your POS. Stale availability gets read back to callers at speed. Garbage in, confident garbage out.

Compliance is plan-dependent: SOC 2, HIPAA & GDPR compliance sits on the Enterprise plan, not the entry tier. If you take card details over the phone or operate in a regulated context, confirm the tier before comparing headline prices.

## How to evaluate it in a week

Do not run a demo and decide. Run your own calls.

1. Export missed and after-hours calls for seven days from your phone provider.
2. Multiply that count by your average ticket to get your own recovery number.
3. Ask what happens to a call the AI cannot resolve, and where it goes.
4. Test one modifier-heavy order and one reservation change.
5. Get the overage rate in writing before signing.

Step two is the one most buyers skip, and it is the only number that tells you whether this pays for itself at your restaurant rather than at a hypothetical one.

## Questions to put to any vendor

Ask how the system handles a caller who changes their mind mid-order, and what the failover is when the AI is unsure. Ask whether the voice is disclosed as automated, because some jurisdictions require it. Ask who owns the call recordings and how long they are retained.
